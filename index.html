01Defect Description

LinkedIn does not recognize the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires (CABA) as an independent geographic entity. The platform categorizes CABA neighborhoods and communes under the "Province of Buenos Aires", even though these are politically and administratively distinct jurisdictions.

Documented example: the Liniers neighborhood (Commune 9 of CABA) is labeled as "Liniers, Commune 9, Province of Buenos Aires".

🏙️ CABA

Federal capital of Argentina. Autonomous status equivalent to a province. Its administrative divisions are called Communes (48 neighborhoods, 15 communes).

🗺️ Province of Buenos Aires

Separate jurisdiction. Capital: La Plata. Its administrative divisions are called Partidos and Municipalities.

⚠️ Report Status

This defect was reported to LinkedIn technical support through official channels. The response was inadequate: it was not classified as a bug and no resolution was provided.

03Commercial and User Experience Risks

Premium users invest money monthly or annually to maximize profile visibility and quality. The defect prevents correctly completing one of the most basic and critical fields: location.

As a LinkedIn Premium user, I consider it a serious flaw that the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires is not listed among the cities and/or provinces of Argentina. I am paying to make my résumé as strong as possible, and it is impossible to state where I live when I do not live in the Province of Buenos Aires but in the City of Buenos Aires.

07Requirements Document — Argentina API v1

Formal technical specification for LinkedIn product and engineering teams to fix the geolocation API defect.

📄 Audience

LinkedIn product and engineering · Module: User Profile → Experience → Location field · Suggested priority: Medium-High · Status: Proposed

Executive Summary

The location typeahead field in the experience form does not correctly distinguish the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires (CABA) from the Province of Buenos Aires (PBA). The system returns ambiguous or incorrect suggestions and in some cases persists free text instead of a canonical geographic entity.

The issue affects three areas: data integrity, user experience, and product trust.

Business Context — Argentina

RuleDescription
CABA ≠ PBACABA is an autonomous federal district; it is NOT part of the Province of Buenos Aires.
AmbiguityThe term "Buenos Aires" without clarification is ambiguous → the system must disambiguate, not assume PBA.
CABA nomenclatureDivisions: 48 neighborhoods, 15 communes (term exclusive to CABA).
PBA nomenclatureCapital: La Plata. Divisions: partidos and municipalities.
CABA acronymMust resolve to the Argentine entity, not international homonyms.
AMBA / GBAMetropolitan area ≠ full province; does not replace CABA as an entity.

Required Entity Model

Acceptance Criteria

  • API: "Ciudad Autónoma de Buenos Aires" must return at least one canonical suggestion with non-null entityId.
  • API: "Buenos Aires" must return separate candidates for CABA and PBA with distinct IDs and disambiguation labels.
  • API: Neighborhoods and communes such as Palermo, Villa Riachuelo, and Commune 9 must resolve under parent jurisdiction CABA.
  • UI: User must navigate suggestions with keyboard and select without mouse (WAI-ARIA accessibility).
  • Persistence: On save, canonical entityId must persist, not free text only.
  • Regression: PBA localities and partidos that currently resolve correctly must continue to do so.

Reference Benchmark — Argentina Georef API

The Argentine government publishes the Georef API as the official reference for territorial normalization. It distinguishes jurisdictions, resolves localities, and supports reverse geocoding.
